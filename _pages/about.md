My work sits at the meeting point between engineering, geography, environmental observation and cultural heritage. I am interested in how new surveying technologies can help us understand places more clearly, measure how they change over time and support better decisions.

Much of my research is carried out in real-world settings: along changing coastlines, across areas affected by subsidence or landslides, beneath the sea, and within historic buildings and infrastructures. These activities involve field surveys, UAVs and underwater platforms, satellite observations, terrestrial measurements and the development of digital models that can be explored, analysed and updated.

I am particularly interested in turning complex observations into useful and accessible information. This includes creating reliable geospatial workflows, combining different types of measurements, developing 3D and immersive representations, and making results understandable to researchers, professionals, students and the wider public.

Teaching is an important part of this work. Through courses and practical activities, I introduce students and professionals to geomatics, GIS, remote sensing, QGIS, open data and 3D documentation. I enjoy connecting theoretical concepts with hands-on exercises and with the kinds of problems that arise in engineering and environmental applications.

On this website, you can find an overview of my research projects, publications, teaching activities, academic background and selected resources. I will also use it to share updates, project results, datasets, software and other material that may be useful to colleagues, students and collaborators.
